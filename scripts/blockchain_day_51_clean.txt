Day 51 Zero-Knowledge Proofs: ZK-SNARKs & ZK-STARKs Foundations (Circuits, R1CS & Groth16) Stage 4: Autonomous Production Readiness Month 2 • Block 11 • 4 Quests 🎯 Spoken Goal & Invariant: Goal: Enter the pinnacle of modern applied cryptography: Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs). Understand the magic: proving knowledge of a secret (the 'witness' w) satisfying a computation C(x, w) = true WITHOUT revealing anything about the secret itself! Trace the mathematical transformation pipeline: 1) High-level computation → 2) Arithmetic Circuit (addition and multiplication gates over finite fields); 3) Rank-1 Constraint System (R1CS: equations of form (A · s) × (B · s) = (C · s)); 4) Quadratic Arithmetic Programs (QAP: converting constraints to polynomial equations); and 5) Groth16 zk-SNARK pairing cryptography. Contrast SNARKs (succinct, constant 200-byte proof, requires trusted setup, vulnerable to quantum computing) with STARKs (transparent setup, quantum-resistant, larger 50-100KB proof sizes). Invariant: Zero-Knowledge & Soundness Invariant: A zero-knowledge proof guarantees Completeness (an honest prover always convinces the verifier), Soundness (a dishonest prover cannot forge a proof except with negligible probability ε ≈ 2^-128), and Zero-Knowledge (the proof leaks zero information beyond statement validity). ⚠️ Failure Mode & tryIt Bug: Failure: Omitting a constraint in an R1CS arithmetic circuit (e.g. failing to constrain that a signal is a binary 0 or 1: 'b * (1 - b) === 0'); an attacker passes an arbitrary integer value (e.g. 5), satisfying the circuit while forging unauthorized proofs to drain privacy pools. tryIt Friction: Construct a minimal R1CS constraint system in TypeScript: represent the computation x^3 + x + 5 = 35; decompose into multiplication gates; generate vectors A, B, C; solve for witness vector s; verify (A · s) × (B · s) = (C · s); and prove that any invalid witness fails constraint evaluation. 🪜 3-Tier Socratic Hint Ladder: [1. What cryptographic primitive was missing from the privacy circuit?" Correct: Option B) Missing Cryptographic Nullifier: ZK proofs are stateless! The circuit proved knowledge of a valid deposit, but did not reveal WHICH deposit was spent! To prevent double-spending in privacy systems (like Tornado Cash or Zcash), the circuit must output a deterministic 'Nullifier Hash' = hash(secret, leafIndex)! The on-chain contract marks the nullifier as spent. If a proof presents an already-spent nullifier, it reverts! • Debrief: In zero-knowledge privacy systems, the protocol cannot track balances by account address (because addresses are hidden!). Double-spending is prevented via 'Nullifiers'. The circuit computes a unique deterministic nullifier hash derived from the secret key. The smart contract stores a list of spent nullifiers, permanently rejecting any proof attempting to reuse an existing nullifier.
