## Pre-screen "likely wrong" items

1. Day 2 Part 4, "Aronson v. Lewis Demand Futility Doctrine ... majority of the board financially interested": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The current test comes from United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg, 262 A.3d 1034 (Del. Sept. 23, 2021), which replaced Aronson and Rales. It asks three questions about each director: did they get a material personal benefit, do they face a substantial likelihood of liability, or do they lack independence from someone who did. Demand is excused if the answer is yes for at least half of the demand board. Note: the pre-screen's citation "261 A.3d 1251" is wrong; the correct cite is 262 A.3d 1034. Sources: courts.delaware.gov (No. 404, 2020); https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/?p=140674 ; https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/supreme-court/2021/404-2020.html. FIXED (S5, S9, S11 and the lab label).
2. Day 2 Part 4, "exception under the Aronson v. Lewis demand futility doctrine": CONFIRMED-WRONG, same as item 1. FIXED.
3. Day 55 Part 4, "Rs 1,450 crore exceeds the SEBI LODR Reg 23(1) absolute cap of Rs 1,000 crore": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The SEBI (LODR) (Fifth Amendment) Regulations 2025 (notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2025/273, November 2025) added Schedule XII, in force from 18 Dec 2025. The materiality threshold is now graded by turnover:
   - turnover up to Rs 20,000 cr: 10% of turnover
   - turnover of Rs 20,000-40,000 cr: Rs 2,000 cr plus 5% of turnover above Rs 20,000 cr
   - turnover above Rs 40,000 cr: Rs 3,000 cr plus 2.5% of turnover above Rs 40,000 cr, capped at Rs 5,000 cr

   For Rs 32,000 cr turnover the threshold is Rs 2,600 cr, so Rs 1,450 cr is not material. Sources: https://foxmandal.in/sebis-fifth-lodr-amendment-2025/ ; https://www.maheshwariandco.com/press-release/sebi-notifies-fifth-amendment-to-lodr-revised-materiality-framework-for-related-party-transactions/ (these two agree; taxguru phrases the middle slab as "higher of", which a lawyer should check against the gazette). FIXED. The Day 55 contract figures were raised by Rs 1,500 cr (to 2,950 and 2,820; the Rs 130 cr overcharge is unchanged) so the scenario stays material, and the labs were re-run. The same outdated rule was also fixed on Days 33, 35 and 54 (see EXTRA).
4. Day 58 Part 4, "Vijay Karia v Cantier Sistemi SpA (2020)": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The correct name is Vijay Karia v. Prysmian Cavi E Sistemi SRL, (2020) 11 SCC 1 (Supreme Court of India, 13 Feb 2020). The holding as taught is right. Source: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2020/07/04/enforcement-of-foreign-award-under-the-arbitration-and-conciliation-act-1996-case-analysis-of-vijay-karia-v-prysmian-cavi-e-sistemi-srl/ ; azbpartners.com (Feb 2020). FIXED (Day 48 already had the correct name).
5. Day 59 Part 2, "Subhkam Ventures (I) Private Limited v SEBI (2011)": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The SAT order is dated 15 Jan 2010 (Appeal No. 8 of 2009). The Supreme Court disposed of SEBI's appeal (CA 3371/2010), left the question of law open, and said the SAT order is not a precedent. The lesson's claim that this was "due to subsequent settlement" could not be verified and was removed. Sources: https://indiacorplaw.in/2010/01/17/relief-for-investor-community-on/ ; https://www.barandbench.com/news/sc-says-sat-order-subhkam-ventures-not-precedent-control-and-veto-rights-war-continues. FIXED (the lesson now says "SAT, January 2010").
6. Day 39 Part 4, "subsequent constitutional bench rulings in ... Raman Ispat": CONFIRMED-WRONG. Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam v Raman Ispat, 2023 INSC 625 (17 July 2023), judgment by S. Ravindra Bhat J., is not a constitution bench decision. It held that the dues of a statutory corporation are financial or operational debt, not s.53(1)(e) government dues. It also confined Rainbow Papers to its facts (a CIRP case, not a liquidation). Sources: https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Raman-Ispat-Supreme-Court-confines-applicability-of-Rainbow-Papers ; 2023 LiveLaw (SC) 534. FIXED. The second judge (Dipankar Datta J. per the pre-screen) was not confirmed, so the lesson does not name the bench.
7. Day 47 Part 2, "In re Betcorp Ltd (396 B.R. 779) ... independent commission agent / human means": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The correct citation is In re Betcorp Ltd., 400 B.R. 266 (Bankr. D. Nev. Feb. 9, 2009, Markell J.). The court recognised an Australian voluntary winding up as a foreign main proceeding and measured COMI as at the date of the recognition petition. The opinion says nothing about "human means" or commission agents. Source: https://jmargolin.com/zandian/usbc/betcorp.htm (opinion text); https://leagle.com/decision/inbco20090209381. FIXED. The Betcorp sentence now states only the verified holding, and the "human means" point is framed as the lesson's reading of Article 2(f). ## Pre-screen "unsure" items

8. Day 2 Part 5, Revlon "sole fiduciary duty is to maximize the immediate cash price": CONFIRMED-WRONG. Under Revlon, 506 A.2d 173, 182 (Del. 1986), directors become "auctioneers charged with getting the best price" and cannot play favourites among bidders. Paramount v. QVC, 637 A.2d 34 (Del. 1994) calls this the "best value reasonably available", which need not mean immediate cash. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/supreme-court/1994/637-a-2d-34-5.html. FIXED (narration, correct quiz option reworded, quiz explanation).
9. Day 3 Part 3, Akorn "Delaware Supreme Court upheld an MAE termination for the first time": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The Court of Chancery (Laster V.C., 1 Oct 2018) made the first Delaware MAE finding, and the Delaware Supreme Court affirmed (No. 535, 2018). Sources: https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2018/ca-2018-0300-jtl.html ; https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2018/10/22/a-watershed-development-for-material-adverse-effect-clauses/. FIXED.
10. Day 3 Part 3, Akorn "persistent 50%+ collapse ... spanning multiple years": CONFIRMED-WRONG. EBITDA fell 86% in the year after signing; the decline lasted about a year, not several. The problems were regulatory data-integrity violations. Sources: as item 9, plus the White & Case client alert. FIXED (S3, S9). Not fixed, for a lawyer to check: S1's ">= 24 months" duration benchmark is not a rule stated in IBP, Hexion or Akorn.
11. Day 11 Part 4, "Under Section 4, only one transaction is exempt": CORRECT. In context it means that only the particular transaction is exempt, as opposed to a Section 3 securities exemption. The wording could be clearer, but it is not wrong. NOT CHANGED.
12. Day 14 Part 1, Kardon (1946) "affirmed by the Supreme Court in Blue Chip Stamps (1975)": CORRECT in substance. Blue Chip, 421 U.S. 723, 730, recognised the implied 10b-5 action; the earlier recognition was Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life, 404 U.S. 6, 13 n.9 (1971). NOT CHANGED. Optional: name Bankers Life.
13. Day 32 Part 4, CSR set-off "s.135(5) third proviso introduced by 2020 amendment": CORRECT. The "Provided also" proviso came in with the Companies (Amendment) Act 2020, and the three-year limit is in CSR Policy Rule 7(3) (amended 22 Jan 2021, with a Board resolution required). Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in copy of s.135; https://iepf.gov.in/content/csr/global/master/home/helpandfaqs.html ; vinodkothari.com. NOT CHANGED.
14. Day 36 Part 5, K. Kishan v. Vijay Nirman (2018): CORRECT. K. Kishan v. Vijay Nirman Co., (2018) 17 SCC 662 (14 Aug 2018) holds that a pending s.34 challenge makes the operational debt disputed. NOT CHANGED. The pre-screen's point about later narrowing (Kay Bouvet, 2021) was not separately verified.
15. Day 38 Part 1, Phoenix ARC "related parties who assign their debts to third parties ... remain disqualified": CONFIRMED-WRONG as framed. Phoenix ARC v Spade Financial (SC, 1 Feb 2021) read the s.21(2) first proviso purposively. A creditor that ceases to be a related party in order to get around the bar is still excluded. The case did not hold that assignees of related-party debt are excluded. Source: https://www.barandbench.com/columns/section-21-ibc-supreme-court-purposive-interpretation-ex-related-parties-coc ; ibbi.gov.in (judgment). FIXED here and on Day 40 Part 2 (narration plus the quiz reframed from "assignment" to "ceasing to be a related party"; the correct option is unchanged).
16. Day 39 Part 5, "Swiss Ribbons and Chitra Sharma ... any transaction to circumvent s.29A is void ab initio": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The anti-circumvention, substance-over-form reading of s.29A comes from ArcelorMittal India v Satish Kumar Gupta (SC, 4 Oct 2018). Swiss Ribbons (2019) upheld s.29A. Neither case holds that such transactions are "void ab initio". Source: https://indiacorplaw.in/2018/10/07/essar-steel-case-supreme-decodes-section-29a-ibc/ ; ibbi.gov.in judgment PDF. FIXED.
17. Day 40 Part 1, "absolute outer ceiling of 330 days ... unbendable ... automatic liquidation": CONFIRMED-WRONG. In CoC of Essar Steel v Satish Kumar Gupta (SC, 15 Nov 2019) the Court struck down the word "mandatorily" in the s.12(3) proviso. 330 days is the ordinary limit, but it may be exceeded in exceptional cases. Source: https://www.bloombergquint.com/law-and-policy/essar-steel-supreme-court-on-when-the-330-day-ibc-deadline-can-be-relaxed. FIXED (narration, quiz question wording, quiz explanation; the correct option is unchanged). Not fixed, for a lawyer to check: any 2025-26 IBC amendment to s.12 (none verified).
18. Day 40 Part 4, Raman Ispat "overriding constitutional supremacy over all ... statutes under s.238": CONFIRMED-WRONG (overstated). The case reaffirmed that the IBC's s.53 waterfall prevails over the Electricity Act charge in liquidation. Source: as item 6. FIXED.
19. Day 45 Part 4, s.32A "absolute immunity": CONFIRMED-WRONG. The immunity applies only if control passes to persons who were not promoters or related parties and did not abet the offence, and officers in default remain liable. Manish Kumar v Union of India (2021) upheld s.32A. Source: IBC s.32A text (ibbi.gov.in). FIXED (narration and correct quiz option). The same wording was also fixed on Day 40 Parts 1 and 4.
20. Day 48 Part 4, Rubin v Eurofinance as an Article 6 public-policy case: CONFIRMED-WRONG. In Rubin v Eurofinance; New Cap v Grant [2012] UKSC 46 (24 Oct 2012, Lord Collins), the foreign avoidance judgments (US in Rubin, Australian in New Cap) were enforceable in England only under the common-law Dicey rule. CBIR 2006 does not change that. New Cap was enforced because the defendants had submitted to the Australian court. Sources: https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2012/46 ; https://maitlandchambers.com/resources/case-detail/eurofinance-sa-ors-v-rubin-anor-2012. FIXED. Day 45 Part 4 said English courts "recognize foreign clawback claims" under Rubin, which contradicts the case; also FIXED.
21. Day 49 Part 1, SBI v Videocon "(2019)", "NCLT Mumbai Bench and NCLAT": STILL-UNSURE in part. The NCLT Mumbai order of 8 Aug 2019 consolidating 13 of the 15 companies is the seminal order, so the year is correct. What a lawyer must check: what the NCLAT decided on consolidation, if anything, and the number of criteria. The course says "9-factor" on Day 49 and "7-pronged" on Day 42, which is internally inconsistent. Source: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/01/09/sbi-v-videocon-case-doctrine-of-substantial-consolidation/ ; bloombergquint.com. NOT CHANGED.
22. Day 54 Part 5, s.188(5) "Rs 25 lakh": CORRECT. After the Companies (Amendment) Act 2020 (s.39, in force 21 Dec 2020), a director or employee is liable to Rs 25 lakh for a listed company and Rs 5 lakh for other companies, as the labs already model. Source: lexplosion.in summary; MCA "Penalties and Offences under Companies Act 2013". NOT CHANGED. A lawyer may confirm against the gazette text.
23. Day 56 Part 1, Hindustan Lever v State of Maharashtra "(2004)": CORRECT. Decided 18 Nov 2003 and reported (2004) 9 SCC 438 (Lahoti and Bhan JJ.). It held that the court order sanctioning an amalgamation is an "instrument" for stamp duty. The decision was under the 1956 Act (s.391-394). Source: https://www.legalauthority.in/judgement/hindustan-lever-vs-state-of-maharashtra-19456 ; indiankanoon doc 1859141. NOT CHANGED.
24. Day 56 Part 2, CCI v Thomas Cook "minor pre-merger steps ... integrating customer databases": CONFIRMED-WRONG as framed. The Supreme Court (17 Apr 2018) upheld a Rs 1 crore s.43A penalty for open-market purchases of Sterling Holiday shares that were part of a composite combination and were made before notifying the CCI. The case says nothing about customer databases. Source: https://www.competitionpolicyinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Recent-India-S.C.-Decision.pdf ; https://www.vantageasia.com/fine-for-market-purchases-casts-doubt-on-exemption/. FIXED.
25. Day 56 Part 4, s.9(1)(i) Income Tax Act 1961: CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 Apr 2026; the 1961 Act still governs earlier years. Source: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/income-tax-act-2025-comes-into-force-from-1st-april-2026. FIXED with a dated note. The new section numbers were not added because they were not verified.
26. Day 56 Part 5, s.50B Income Tax Act 1961: CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated), same as item 25. FIXED with a day-level note in Part 1 (S0). The Part 3 and Part 5 mentions keep the 1961 numbering under that note. Not fixed, for a lawyer to check: the new 2025-Act section number for slump sales.
27. Day 58 Part 3, Vedanta Article 137 "(i.e., when the foreign award is delivered to the party)": CONFIRMED-WRONG (unsupported gloss). Government of India v Vedanta (SC, 16 Sep 2020) holds that the three-year limit runs "from when the right to apply accrues". Commentators say the Court did not fix award delivery as the trigger. Source: https://arbitrationblog.kluwerarbitration.com/2020/10/18/limitation-period-for-enforcement-of-foreign-award-supreme-court-of-india-finally-answers/ ; https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/arbitration/2020-12/recent-developments-in-india-related-arbitration-3. FIXED (gloss removed).

29. EXTRA, Day 17 Part 3 and Day 20 Part 2, DGCL §144: CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). SB 21 (85 Del. Laws c. 6, effective 25 Mar 2025) rewrote §144. §144(a) now bars equitable relief and damages rather than only voidability. Route (a)(1) requires good faith and no gross negligence, with a two-director committee route. Route (a)(2) requires a majority of votes cast by disinterested stockholders. Benihana (2006) is kept as history. Source: https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc04/index.html ; https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/04/01/delaware-enacts-important-corporate-law-reforms/. FIXED (quiz correct options unchanged).
30. EXTRA, Day 23 Part 1, controller definition and Tornetta status: CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). §144(e) defines a controlling stockholder as one with majority voting power, a right to elect a board majority, or at least one-third of the voting power plus managerial authority. Tornetta: on 19 Dec 2025 the Delaware Supreme Court (per curiam) reversed the rescission remedy, reinstated the 2018 package and awarded $1 in nominal damages. It did not reach liability or the controller finding. Fee award: quantum meruit with a 4x multiplier. Sources: delcode as above; https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/12/delaware-supreme-court-restores-musks-2018-pay-package ; https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/delaware-supreme-court-reverses-court-of-chancerys-rescission-of-elon-musks-2018-tesla-ceo-equity-award. FIXED (narration and quiz; the "20-30%" correct option was changed to "at least one-third for §144").
31. EXTRA, Day 23 Part 3, "As the lesson reads Tornetta v. Musk, post-hoc conditions cannot cure a missing ab initio commitment": CONFIRMED-WRONG. Tornetta is not an MFW timing case; the timing rule comes from Flood v. Synutra (Del. 2018). §144(c) now sets the statutory going-private safe harbor. FIXED.
32. EXTRA, Day 25 Part 3, Match Group "extends the same requirement to any conflicted transaction": CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). FIXED (the quiz on take-privates is still correct).
