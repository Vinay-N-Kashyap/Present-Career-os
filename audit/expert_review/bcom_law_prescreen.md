## Likely wrong

1. **Day 2, Part 4** (two items: "Aronson v. Lewis Demand Futility Doctrine ... majority of the board financially interested" and "exception under the Aronson v. Lewis demand futility doctrine")
   - Correct: Delaware Supreme Court in United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg (Del. 2021, 261 A.3d 1251) replaced the Aronson and Rales tests with one three-part, director-by-director test. Demand is excused if at least half of the current board (1) received a material personal benefit from the alleged misconduct, (2) faces a substantial likelihood of liability, or (3) lacks independence from someone who does. Aronson's second prong (business judgment rule not available) was dropped. Teach Zuckerberg, with Aronson/Rales as history.
   - Source: https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/09/28/delaware-supreme-court-announces-new-demand-futility-test ; https://www.courts.delaware.gov (Zuckerberg opinion, No. 404, 2020).

2. **Day 55, Part 4** - "RPT of Rs 1,450 crore exceeds the SEBI LODR Reg 23(1) absolute cap of Rs 1,000 crore (10% of Rs 32,000 crore turnover = 3,200; Rs 1,000 crore cap applies)"
   - Correct: SEBI (LODR) Fifth Amendment Regulations 2025 (notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2025/273, dated 18 Nov 2025, RPT provisions effective 18 Dec 2025) replaced the "lower of Rs 1,000 crore or 10% of turnover" test with graded thresholds in a new Schedule XII: turnover up to Rs 20,000 crore: 10% of turnover; Rs 20,000-40,000 crore: Rs 2,000 crore + 5% of turnover above Rs 20,000 crore; above Rs 40,000 crore: Rs 3,000 crore + 2.5% of turnover above Rs 40,000 crore, capped at Rs 5,000 crore. For Rs 32,000 crore turnover the threshold is about Rs 2,600 crore, so a Rs 1,450 crore contract is NOT material under the new test. (Figures from secondary summaries; the lawyer should confirm against Schedule XII text.)
   - Source: https://foxmandal.in/sebis-fifth-lodr-amendment-2025/ ; https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-updates-rpt-materiality-thresholds-and-approval-norms/ ; SEBI notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2025/273 (sebi.gov.in).

3. **Day 58, Part 4** - "Vijay Karia v Cantier Sistemi SpA (2020)"
   - Correct: Vijay Karia v. Prysmian Cavi e Sistemi SRL (Supreme Court of India, 13 Feb 2020, 2020 INSC 178). The party name "Cantier Sistemi SpA" is wrong. Holding (narrow Section 48 grounds, no merits review) is right.
   - Source: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2020/07/04/enforcement-of-foreign-award-under-the-arbitration-and-conciliation-act-1996-case-analysis-of-vijay-karia-v-prysmian-cavi-e-sistemi-srl/

4. **Day 59, Part 2** - "Subhkam Ventures (I) Private Limited v SEBI (2011)"
   - Correct: the SAT order is dated 15 January 2010 (Appeal No. 8 of 2009), not 2011. Also note SEBI appealed and the Supreme Court (Civil Appeal 3371 of 2010) disposed of it leaving the question of law open, so the SAT order is not binding Supreme Court precedent on "control".
   - Source: https://indiacorplaw.in/2010/01/17/relief-for-investor-community-on/ ; https://www.barandbench.com/news/sc-says-sat-order-subhkam-ventures-not-precedent-control-and-veto-rights-war-continues

5. **Day 39, Part 4** - "subsequent constitutional bench rulings in Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam v. Raman Ispat"
   - Correct: decided 17 July 2023 (2023 INSC 625, Civil Appeal 7976 of 2019) by a two-judge bench (S. Ravindra Bhat and Dipankar Datta JJ.), not a constitution bench. Holding: security interest of a statutory electricity corporation (not the Central/State Government) is treated as financial/operational debt, not "government dues", and Section 53 waterfall prevails over the Electricity Act. The lesson's description of government dues as tier five (Section 53(1)(e)) is a separate point, but the "constitutional bench" wording and the "tax dues" framing should be corrected.
   - Source: https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/534-paschimanchal-vidyut-vitran-nigam-ltd-v-raman-ispat-pvt-ltd-17-jul-2023-482015.pdf ; https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Raman-Ispat-Supreme-Court-confines-applicability-of-Rainbow-Papers

6. **Day 47, Part 2** - "In re Betcorp Ltd (396 B.R. 779)"
   - Correct citation: In re Betcorp Ltd., 400 B.R. 266 (Bankr. D. Nev. 2009). Separately, the described holding (independent commission agent not satisfying "human means" of Article 2(f)) could not be confirmed; Betcorp is usually cited for COMI being measured at the date of the recognition petition and not resting on the registered-office presumption. Lawyer should check the proposition for Betcorp and Ackers v Saad [2010] FCA 1221.
   - Source: https://leagle.com/decision/inbco20090209381 ; https://jmargolin.com/zandian/usbc/betcorp.htm

## Unsure

- **Day 2, Part 5** (Revlon): "sole fiduciary duty is to maximize the immediate cash price per share" - Revlon requires a good-faith effort to obtain the best value reasonably available, not necessarily "immediate cash"; stock-for-stock sales and the Lyondell/Paramount line affect this. Check wording.
- **Day 3, Part 3** (two Akorn items): Chancery (Laster, V.C., Oct 2018) found the first Delaware MAE; Delaware Supreme Court affirmed (Dec 2018). Check "Supreme Court upheld ... for the first time" wording and the "50%+ ... spanning multiple years" EBITDA description against the opinion.
- **Day 11, Part 4**: "Under Section 4, only one transaction is exempt" - Securities Act s.4 contains several exemptions (e.g. 4(a)(1), 4(a)(2), 4(a)(7)); check the context and whether it means s.4(a)(2).
- **Day 14, Part 1**: Kardon (E.D. Pa. 1946) is correct; check "affirmed by the Supreme Court in Blue Chip Stamps (1975)". Blue Chip recognised the implied action and limited standing to purchasers/sellers; earlier acknowledgement was Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life (1971).
- **Day 32, Part 4**: CSR set-off "Section 135(5) third proviso introduced by 2020 amendment" - the three-year set-off is prescribed in Rule 7(3) of the CSR Policy Amendment Rules 2021 (effective 22 Jan 2021) under a proviso to s.135(5); check which proviso number and which Act (2019 vs 2020).
- **Day 36, Part 5**: K. Kishan v. Vijay Nirman (2018) - check that it holds a pending s.34 challenge makes the operational debt "disputed"; later narrowed (e.g. Kay Bouvet, 2021).
- **Day 38, Part 1**: Phoenix ARC v. Spade Financial Services (SC, 1 Feb 2021) - confirmed it excludes related-party financial creditors from the CoC (s.21(2) proviso); check the specific claim about assignment to circumvent s.21(2).
- **Day 39, Part 5**: "In Swiss Ribbons and Chitra Sharma ... any transaction designed to circumvent s.29A is void ab initio" - check holdings (Swiss Ribbons upheld constitutionality of 29A; Chitra Sharma on promoter ineligibility).
- **Day 40, Part 1**: 180 + 90 days, 330-day outer limit - check against any 2025/2026 IBC amendments and Ebix/Essar Steel treatment as directory.
- **Day 40, Part 4**: Raman Ispat described as "overriding constitutional supremacy over all conflicting central and state statutes under s.238" - overstated; see item 5 above.
- **Day 45, Part 4**: s.32A "absolute immunity" - Manish Kumar (2021) upheld it, but immunity is conditional (bona fide plan, no promoter/related-party benefit); check wording.
- **Day 48, Part 4**: Rubin v Eurofinance [2012] UKSC 46 concerned common-law enforcement of US avoidance judgments (Dicey Rule 43), not Article 6 public policy under the Model Law; check the lesson's use.
- **Day 49, Part 1**: SBI v Videocon Industries (NCLT Mumbai 2018, NCLAT 2019) - check year and which order is the "seminal" one.
- **Day 54, Part 5**: s.188(5) "Rs 25 lakh statutory penalty" - post-Companies (Amendment) Act 2020 text (effective 21 Dec 2020) differs for listed vs other companies (secondary source shows 25 lakh for listed company directors/employees, 5 lakh other companies); check statute text.
- **Day 56, Part 1**: Hindustan Lever v State of Maharashtra "(2004)" - check year and that it is the "landmark" s.230 authority (case is a stamp duty/amalgamation order decision, (2003/2004) 9 SCC 438).
- **Day 56, Part 2**: CCI v Thomas Cook (India) (SC 2018) - check that it concerns "minor pre-merger steps" under s.43A; the case is on gun-jumping / s.6(2A) in composite transactions.

The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect 1 April 2026 and replaces the 1961 Act (1961 Act still governs assessment year 2026-27 and earlier); section numbers changed. Check wording as "1961 Act (as applicable to earlier years)" and new section numbers.
- **Day 58, Part 3**: Article 137 Limitation Act, three years for s.47 enforcement (Government of India v Vedanta, 2020); check "when the foreign award is delivered" as the accrual point.
