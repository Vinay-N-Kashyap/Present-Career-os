# B.Com Taxation - expert verdicts on the pre-screen (2026-11-26)

Scope: the 9 "likely wrong" and the "unsure" items in `bcom_tax_prescreen.md`, checked against statute text (incometaxindia.gov.in section pages, Finance Act / Bill text, egazette) first, then at least two independent secondary sources. Fixes are in `claude_sandbox/expand/fix/bcom_tax.expert.ops.json` (86 edits, dry run 0 problems, not yet written). Part numbers below are the checklist's (1-based); the ops file uses 0-based part indexes.

Verdicts: CONFIRMED-WRONG = the lesson states a wrong or outdated rule (fixed unless noted). CORRECT = the pre-screen was wrong or over-cautious. STILL-UNSURE = a CA must check what is stated.

## Likely wrong (9)

1. D3 P3, US 25% / UK 15% - CONFIRMED-WRONG (as statutory rates). US federal corporate rate is 21% (IRC s.11(b)); UK main rate is 25% from 1 Apr 2023. The lab arithmetic needs the lesson's figures, so they are now labelled as illustrative teaching rates, and the real rates are stated. Source: IRC s.11; gov.uk Corporation Tax rates. FIXED (say + example).
2. D14 P1, buyback "paradigm shift" of FA 2024 presented as current - CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). Deemed dividend under s.2(22)(f) applied 1 Oct 2024 to 31 Mar 2026. Finance Act 2026 taxes buyback proceeds as capital gains in the shareholder's hands from 1 Apr 2026 (tax year 2026-27), with an extra tax on promoters. Sources: businesstoday.in (Finance Act 2026 explainer, 15 Apr 2026; "three times in 18 months", 3 Sep 2026); taxguru.in "buyback tax reset Finance Act 2026"; scconline.com blog 16 Jul 2026. FIXED (time-anchored).
3. D14 P5, s.2(22)(f) + s.46A capital loss - CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law (correct for 1 Oct 2024 to 31 Mar 2026). From 1 Apr 2026, cost is deducted in computing the gain and there is no separate capital loss. Sources: as item 2; elplaw.in Budget Buzz (Feb 2026). FIXED (narration, quiz question, why, all time-anchored; correct option unchanged).
4. D14 P5, $645.8M dividend tax / $1,200M capital loss example - CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law. Arithmetic is kept as the 2024-26 regime with its dates, plus one sentence on the 2026 rule. FIXED.
5. D22 P5, FA 2021 "abolished" s.153A and moved all search cases to s.147/148 - CONFIRMED-WRONG. FA 2021 stopped s.153A for searches from 1 Apr 2021. For searches on or after 1 Sep 2024, the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 brought back block assessment (Chapter XIV-B, s.158BA onwards). Sections 153A-153D were search assessment provisions, not "block assessment". Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-158bg-30 and section-158ba-30; taxmann.com block assessment analysis; EY alert Aug 2024; Deloitte Budget 2024 procedural note (saving for searches 1 Apr 2021 to 31 Aug 2024). FIXED: the example search moved from Nov 2024 to Jun 2024 (same FY/AY, lab output unchanged), and the quiz is time-anchored.
6. D33 P5, s.165A 2% levy stated as current - CONFIRMED-WRONG. s.165A(4) (Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 s.165) switches it off for e-commerce supplies on or after 1 Aug 2024. The 6% levy on online advertising was withdrawn from 1 Apr 2025 (Finance Act 2025). Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-165a; caclubindia/taxscan Budget 2024 notes; business-standard.com 1 Apr 2025. FIXED (EL 1.0, EL 2.0 and Pillar One sentences).
7. D39 P3, s.14A IGST 28% GST on "full face value of bets" - CONFIRMED-WRONG (outdated). 28% applied from 1 Oct 2023 (Notification 11/2023-CT(Rate)). It became 40% from 22 Sep 2025 after the 56th GST Council (CBIC rate notifications of 17 Sep 2025). The value is the amount paid or deposited by the player (Rule 31B CGST Rules). The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 now prohibits online money games. Sources: EY alert Sep 2025 (CBIC notifications for the 56th Council); West Bengal CT dept Sep 2025 rate update; taxguru.in/?p=1081145. FIXED. The PROGA commencement date (sources say 1 May 2026) is not stated in the lesson.
8. D49 P3, s.270AA immunity from s.276CCC - CONFIRMED-WRONG. s.270AA covers prosecution under s.276C and s.276CC (wilful failure to furnish return). Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-270aa; Finance Act 2016 text; itatonline s.270AA article. FIXED (Day 49: narration twice + quiz why; also the same error in Day 24 P3 S0).
9. D55 P4, P. Mohanakala + "Delhi HC" Softbrands: comparables are pure fact under s.260A - CONFIRMED-WRONG. Softbrands is a Karnataka HC ruling, and its view was set aside by the SC in SAP Labs India v. ITO (Apr 2023): the HC examines whether ALP was determined per the Act and Rules, and still interferes only on perversity. P. Mohanakala (SC 2007) is a s.68 cash-credit case. Sources: EY alert Apr 2023 (SAP Labs); taxmann.com SAP Labs post; Khaitan Ergo 24 Apr 2023; RSM newsflash. FIXED (narration, quiz why, and the Day 55 P5 lab narration). The correct quiz option is unchanged and still right.

## Unsure (28 claims under the pre-screen's 20 bullets)

10. D21 P2, s.142(1) proviso three-year limit - rule CORRECT (the proviso's text is absolute, with no senior-officer override). The "landmark SC ruling in CIT v." prefix names no case and is a CONFIRMED-WRONG phantom citation. FIXED (prefix removed). Source: s.142(1) proviso text.
11. D21 P3, Hotel Blue Moon - CORRECT in substance. ACIT v. Hotel Blue Moon (SC, 2 Feb 2010) held that a s.143(2) notice issued within time is mandatory and that its absence is not curable. The case arose in a block assessment (s.158BC), and its title is ACIT v., not CIT v. Title and context corrected in D21 P1/P3 narration. Source: itatonline.org/archives/?p=1362; taxguru summaries. (The Day 25 lab label still reads "CIT v. Hotel Blue Moon"; that is cosmetic and not fixed.)
12. D21 P3, s.143(2) proviso three months - rule CORRECT (FA 2021). Phantom citation prefix CONFIRMED-WRONG, FIXED.
13. D21 P4, verification unit functions in s.144B(2) - CONFIRMED-WRONG. The units and their functions are in s.144B(3) (TOLA 2020 text s.144B(3)(iv); a CBDT order cites s.144B(3)(iii) after FA 2022). FIXED (D21 narration x3 + quiz why; also D46 P2). Source: indiankanoon doc 88315703 (s.144B text); taxguru.in/?p=926378 (CBDT order). A CA should confirm the clause, (iii) or (iv).
14. D21 P5, "third proviso to s.153(1)" for the TP 12-month extension - CONFIRMED-WRONG. The extension is in s.153(4) (FA 2016); the s.153(1) provisos set AY-specific limits. FIXED (narration + quiz why). Source: s.153 text.
15. D22 P1, Ashish Agarwal as authority for the 7-30 day window - CONFIRMED-WRONG. The window was statutory (old s.148A(b), 1 Apr 2021 to 31 Aug 2024). Ashish Agarwal (SC, 4 May 2022, 444 ITR 1) deemed the Apr-Jun 2021 notices to be s.148A(b) notices under Art. 142. FIXED. Sources: s.148A (2021 text); SC judgment summaries.
16. D22 P2, Ashish Agarwal "affirmed s.148A as a mandatory safeguard" - CORRECT in substance. The SC described the 2021 provisions as beneficial protections for assessees and applied them to the old notices. Not edited.
17. D22 P2, s.148A(b) "7 to 30 days" - CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law. It held for notices up to 31 Aug 2024. The substituted s.148A lets the notice specify the reply period. FIXED (narration + quiz question/why time-anchored). Sources: taxguru.in/?p=925203 (Budget 2024 reassessment); businesstoday.in 11 Aug 2024 (s.148A changes).
18-19. D22 P3 (two items), s.149(1)(b) beyond three years, Rs 50 lakh - threshold CORRECT. Phantom citations and the time limits are CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law: for notices from 1 Sep 2024 the limits are 3 years 3 months, and 5 years 3 months for Rs 50 lakh or more (previously 3 and 10 years). FIXED. The lab cases give the same results under both laws and are now explained under the 2024 limits. Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-149-63 (text quoted); ELP Budget Buzz Jul 2024; itatonline "A stitch in time" (Oct 2024).
20. D22 P4, s.151(ii) PCCIT/CCIT tier - CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law. From 1 Sep 2024 there is a single specified authority (Addl. CIT / Addl. Director / JCIT / JDIT). FIXED: rule time-anchored, example moved to AY 2019-20 reopened in Jul 2024 (lab output unchanged), quiz time-anchored. Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-151-63; ITAT order quoting the FA 2024 text.
21-22. D22 P5 (two items), "first proviso to s.148A" search exemption - CONFIRMED-WRONG as current law (and phantom citations). It applied to searches 1 Apr 2021 to 31 Aug 2024; later searches go to Chapter XIV-B. FIXED. STILL-UNSURE: whether the old s.148A had a single proviso (so "first" is a misnomer). CA to check.
23. D43 P1, s.25(4A) two-year sunset - CORRECT. It applies only to conditional exemptions (as the lesson says), lapsing on the 31 March after two years. The Finance Act 2023 proviso excludes some categories, and the lesson's "all" slightly overstates. Not edited. Sources: indiankanoon doc 26453934 (s.25 text); businesstoday column 8 Feb 2023; EY alert Feb 2024.
24. D44 P2, Notification 21/2023-Customs - CORRECT. It is the Advance Authorisation exemption dated 1 Apr 2023 under FTP 2023 (companions 22-24/2023). Sources: icmai.in ITN 06-04-2023; EEPC/Texprocil circulars; egazette 244837. CA to confirm the exact duty list (BCD, SWS, IGST, cess) in the current text.
25. D45 P3, s.65 / MOOWR DTA clearance base - STILL-UNSURE. The core rule is correct: deferred duty on the imported inputs, plus GST on the finished goods. A CA should check the Finance Act 2025 proviso to s.65(1) (power to exclude classes, and reported IGST/cess changes). Sources: grantthornton.in MOOWR articles; taxguru MOOWR note.
26. D47 P3, FA 2022 amended s.149(1)(b) - CORRECT (widened "asset" to include expenditure and book entries, w.e.f. 1 Apr 2022). The ten-year language in Day 47 P3 is time-anchored, and its s.151 two-tier quiz is anchored to before 1 Sep 2024: otherwise option 1 (JCIT/Addl. CIT) would now be the right answer. FIXED (anchors).
27. D47 P3, first proviso to s.149(1) (no revival of years barred under pre-2021 law) - CORRECT for the 2021 text. CA to confirm the proviso numbering in the 2024 substituted s.149.
28. D49 P4, s.271AAD by FA 2020, "fortified" by FA 2021 - CORRECT. Inserted w.e.f. 1 Apr 2020. FA 2021 extended s.281B provisional attachment to s.271AAD penalties above Rs 2 crore; FA 2022 also amended s.271AAD. Not edited. Sources: Finance Bill 2020 cl.98; Finance Act 2021 s.88 (s.281B); Finance Bill 2022.
29. D50 P3, Hexaware (Bombay HC) and Catchy Prop-Build (Delhi HC) - STILL-UNSURE. Hexaware Technologies v. ACIT (Bom HC 2024, 464 ITR 430) is verified, but its holding is that s.148 notices after 29 Mar 2022 must come from the faceless AO (s.151A), which is not quite the lesson's framing. Catchy Prop-Build was not traced. CA to verify both, and any later SC ruling on the JAO/FAO issue.
30. D51 P4, personal hearing in faceless appeals - CONFIRMED-WRONG. Under the Faceless Appeal Scheme 2021 (28 Dec 2021) the CIT(A) "shall" allow a requested VC hearing; CCIT/DG approval was the 2020 scheme. (The pre-screen's own premise was the 2020 rule.) FIXED. Sources: taxmann.com "CIT(A) is bound to allow request for personal hearing"; BDO alert; deccanherald.com; ITAT orders.
31. D51 P4, Babu Ram v. CIT with Manish Build Well on Rule 46A - STILL-UNSURE. CIT v. Manish Build Well (Del HC, 15 Nov 2011) is verified as holding Rule 46A(3) mandatory; "Babu Ram v. CIT" was not traced. CA to verify or remove.
32. D52 P1, Turner International (Delhi HC) "affirmed by the Supreme Court" - STILL-UNSURE. The Delhi HC ruling of 17 May 2017 (WP(C) 4260/2015) is verified; the SC outcome of the revenue's SLP (Diary 10327/2018) is not. "Affirmed" may overstate an SLP dismissal.
33. D52 P1, JCB India v. DCIT (Delhi HC) on s.144C in reassessment - STILL-UNSURE. Not traced.
34. D53 P3, "first proviso to s.254(2A) enacted by Finance Act 2020" - CONFIRMED-WRONG. The provisos were inserted by FA 2001 (w.e.f. 1 Jun 2001) and substituted in 2007, with the third proviso from 2008. FA 2020 (s.99) amended the first proviso to add the 20% deposit/security condition. FIXED. Sources: Finance Bill 2001 and Finance Act 2007/2008 text (taxmanagementindia); Finance Bill 2020.
35. D53 P3, Pepsi Foods (SC, 6 Apr 2021) - CORRECT in substance. The SC held the third proviso (as amended by FA 2008) violative of Art. 14 and allowed stay beyond 365 days where delay is not attributable to the assessee. Technically it struck down/read down the offending words. Not edited.
36. D58 P3, Rule 11UAE "higher of FMV1 and FMV2" and Bharat Bijlee - CORRECT. Rule 11UAE: FMV is the higher of FMV1 and FMV2 on the date of slump sale. CIT v. Bharat Bijlee is a Bombay HC ruling (9 May 2014, ITA 2153/2011): exchange is not sale, so s.50B does not apply. Sources: incometaxindia.gov.in/w/rule-11uae; itatonline Bharat Bijlee PDF; Khaitan note 2021.
37. D59 P4, 2024 s.171 validity case - CORRECT. Reckitt Benckiser India Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India (Delhi HC, Jan 2024, [2024] 158 taxmann.com 675) upheld s.171 and the related Rules. The lesson already names it in full. Sources: taxmann.com; scconline.com 31 Jan 2024; Delhi HC order (Tata Play, 23 Sep 2025).

## Not fixed - for the human CA

- D22 P4: the lesson and lab treat the search AY plus three preceding AYs as "deemed information" years. Explanation 2 to s.148 (2021 text) appears to cover the three AYs immediately preceding the search AY, so the lab's "11 years" count needs a CA check.
- D25 P2 (capstone): the example (AY 2021-22 notice in 2026, PCIT approval, s.151(ii) two-tier) uses pre-Sep 2024 rules. Under current law the 3y3m limit already bars it, and the specified authority is Addl./Joint CIT. The quiz's correct option relies on the old s.151(ii), so it needs reframing (to a pre-Sep 2024 date) by a CA.
- D50 P3: the s.151(ii) PCCIT tier for AY 2018-19, where the notice date is not stated; the D47 P5 lab narration ("extended ten-year authorization").
- D19 P1 S6, D19 P3 S4 and quiz why describe Equalisation Levy in the present tense (D19 P3 S1 already carries a withdrawal note), and D19 Pillar One says India must still repeal the levy.
- Buyback promoter tax: sources disagree on the mechanism (effective 22% corporate / 30% non-corporate promoters vs. an additional-tax or surcharge description), so the lesson states no rate. Also, the 2024 buyback rule was enacted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024; the lesson calls it "Finance Act 2024".
- D33 P5 S1: the claim that foreign companies cannot claim foreign tax credit for Equalisation Levy is an over-generalisation; it depends on the home country.
